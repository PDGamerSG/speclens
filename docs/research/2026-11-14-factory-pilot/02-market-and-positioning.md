# Factory pilot: market evidence and positioning

Research/retrieval date: **2026-11-14, Asia/Kolkata**. This is a bounded public-source comparison of five products, not a market-size study, procurement assessment, patent search, or independent accuracy benchmark.

## Recommendation

Position the first SpecLens factory pilot as **assisted visual inspection for one part family and a controlled specification revision, with reviewed engineer corrections and a traceable human disposition**. Test whether a correction helps on later, unseen factory parts without increasing defect escapes or unnecessary rejection. Keep the quality engineer responsible for final disposition during the pilot.

Do not position “learns from corrections,” “no-code visual inspection,” “continuous improvement,” or “quality traceability” as unique. Official sources already describe these mechanisms. The closest contrary evidence is especially direct:

> “Corrections are validated centrally or locally, then reconciled across every connected machine, so a fix made once reaches your whole fleet.”
>
> Robovision, **[Indurion](https://robovision.ai/indurion)**, “Built for production,” opening paragraph; retrieved 2026-11-14. The requested `/platform` URL redirected to `/indurion`. This is a vendor capability description, not independent evidence of its effectiveness.

SpecLens can investigate a narrower proposition: a quality engineer can turn a specification clause and a visual example into an approved, cited inspection standard, apply it through retrieval on subsequent photos, and inspect its measured benefits and regressions. That proposition is **unvalidated**; this search does not establish that competitors lack the same implementation or that buyers want it.

## Scope and current baseline

The comparison covers LandingAI LandingLens, Roboflow, Instrumental, Overview, and Robovision Indurion. These are relevant alternatives at different layers: model development, application orchestration, manufacturing data/traceability, integrated inspection hardware, and governed vision deployments. They are not equivalent packages or established substitutes for the same factory purchase.

All external capability statements below come from opened official pages or documentation. They show what vendors describe, not whether the products meet a particular defect size, line speed, false-accept target, deployment time, or economic outcome. No vendor prices, customer ROI figures, market size, customer demand, or novelty claims were inferred. Publication dates were not established; retrieval dates and relative “last updated” labels are not publication dates.

The local baseline comes primarily from [README.md](../../../README.md), particularly “Features,” “How it works,” and “How memory is isolated.” SpecLens already has specification import and classes, single/batch inspection, Accept/Reject/Review, saved engineer corrections, Hindsight recall and citations, visual crops, masks and estimated millimetres, paired memory comparison, history, a dashboard, export/delete, and phone capture. These should be reused rather than proposed as new features. [The user guide](../../user-guide.md#accounts-and-sign-in) and [threat model](../../security/threat-model.md#visitor-workspaces) describe browser-cookie isolation and no organizational accounts. Some older guide details differ from the current README; this note is not a code audit and does not treat those older details as authoritative for current feature behavior.

## Table stakes, possible opportunities, and constraints

“Table stakes” here means repeatedly observed capability or an operational prerequisite for the selected pilot. It is not a claim that every factory will buy it.

| Area | Existing SpecLens foundation | Pilot implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definitions and evidence | Imported specification, clauses/classes, reason, defect mark and visual correction crop | Keep a readable specification revision and the exact clause/rule/evidence used for each result. Definition consistency is a competitive baseline [E1, E15]. |
| Human review | Correction form, reviewer name, Review result, batch ordering | A result of “Review” is not a disposition workflow. Identify who must act, what they decided, and what remains pending. Review loops already exist elsewhere [E4, E14–E16]. |
| Controlled change | Memory isolated by exact specification and correction provenance | Pilot reviewers need approved, versioned standards, conflict handling, revocation and reproducible prior results. Evaluation and controlled promotion are competitive baselines [E5–E6, E15]. |
| Traceability | Inspection IDs, history, original/corrected decisions, export | Add factory identifiers and capture context before expanding charts. Instrumental explicitly centers serial-level evidence [E7–E9]. |
| Validation | Same-photo memory on/off comparison and existing benchmark tooling | Reuse these foundations for frozen, independent factory holdouts and temporal transfer. A successful same-photo demonstration does not establish generalization [E5, E12, E15]. |
| Physical station | Phone/upload capture, photo suitability check, size estimates | Establish visible defect coverage and capture consistency. Estimated millimetres do not establish calibrated metrology [E10, E12]. |
| Shared operation | Cookie-isolated public visitor data | A recoverable pilot team workspace, named identities and access boundaries are needed if multiple people share factory records. This is a pilot delivery requirement, not a competitive uniqueness claim. |
| Deployment/integration | Hosted inspection and memory services | Confirm network, data-transfer and cycle-time constraints early. Offline/edge and machine I/O are established alternatives [E2–E3, E10–E11, E14, E16], not defaults that must all be rebuilt. |

The opportunity is to reduce the effort between “the quality engineer explained this exception” and “the next inspection can use a reviewed standard with visible evidence.” Existing alternatives make this an execution and validation hypothesis, not an uncontested product category.

## Concrete positioning hypothesis and falsifying check

**Candidate pilot statement:** “For a quality team inspecting one defined part family, SpecLens keeps reviewed exceptions to a specification with the inspection evidence, applies those standards to later photos, and records the engineer's final decision.”

The current retrieval-based mechanism could offer a shorter correction-to-use loop than collecting labels and retraining a task model. That is an inference about a possible workflow advantage, **not a measured speed advantage or a claim about every competitor**. Roboflow supports multimodal models [E3], and Indurion explicitly supports correction distribution [E14]; the market evidence does not establish an exclusive mechanism.

Falsify the hypothesis with a factory-owned evaluation: choose visible defect classes and a capture protocol; obtain adjudicated ground truth; freeze a memory-off baseline and an approved memory snapshot; compare them on later source parts excluded from corrections and tuning. Separate source-part families, duplicates, lots and time periods to avoid evaluating the taught image or its near-duplicate. Report defective-part false accepts, good-part false rejects, Review/abstention rate, per-class counts, disagreement, reviewer time and end-to-end latency. Show uncertainty and denominators; let the factory choose acceptance limits and defect severity. Repeat comparisons must account for stochastic model behavior and prevent evaluation examples from leaking into memory.

The next discovery check is whether the factory actually has recurring, visually expressible interpretation disagreements tied to a specification. If the bottleneck is an invisible defect, unstable optics, inline speed, or missing process data, correction memory may be the wrong first solution. Failure to improve held-out decisions, regression on previously correct parts, excessive review, or reviewer refusal to trust the evidence would all weaken this positioning.

## Eight candidate pilot features

These are research-backed candidates, not a commitment to build all eight. Start by agreeing the station, identifiers, capture protocol, ground truth and limits; use those facts to constrain the implementation. Reuse existing inspection, memory, history, export, benchmark and batch UI.

| Candidate | Why it belongs in a real pilot | Reuse and dependencies | Validation question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Recoverable team workspace with named pilot roles** | Multiple inspectors and a quality lead need to share records without relying on one browser cookie. Named reviewer/approver identities make later approvals meaningful. | Reuse current workspace scoping and owner checks. Add authenticated membership and server-enforced permissions for the pilot; decide how the pilot data is isolated and recovered. Avoid building a complete enterprise identity suite. | Who can inspect, propose a correction, approve a standard, change a specification, export data or delete records? Can an inspector change role-sensitive state directly through the API? |
| **2. Unit/lot/station inspection context** | A result must refer to the item that was inspected and the conditions under which it was seen. Serial-level evidence is central in Instrumental [E7–E9]. | Extend the existing record with the factory's actual unit/lot, part/SKU, station, operator, capture time and revision identifiers; use manual entry or a simple scan only where useful. Keep repeat inspections linked to the same unit. | Which identifiers already exist, and can staff reliably find a disputed unit and all its inspections without adopting a new numbering system? |
| **3. Named specification and approved standard versions** | A hash isolates data but does not tell a quality lead which revision is in force. Wrong-revision recall and silently changed exceptions are pilot risks. Competitors explicitly document governed data/recipe changes [E5–E6, E15]. | Reuse exact-spec scope isolation and correction IDs. Add human-readable revision, effective state and a standard lifecycle: proposed → approved/active → superseded/revoked. Preserve snapshots, approver and reason; surface conflicting standards for human resolution. | Who owns the authoritative specification? What happens to an active correction when its clause changes, it conflicts with another rule, or an approval is withdrawn? |
| **4. Review and disposition queue** | A model's Accept/Reject/Review and a human's final disposition are different facts. Existing correction UI does not provide assignment, open/closed state or shift handoff. Human review loops are established [E4, E14–E16]. | Reuse batch/history and original-versus-corrected decisions. Add review owner, reason, state, timestamps and adjudicated disposition; preserve the original output and reopening history. Prevent double handling of the same unit. | Can operators clear their real queue, hand off unresolved items, and distinguish “model rejected” from “quality engineer rejected”? What review load is acceptable? |
| **5. Capture protocol and retake guidance** | Imaging is part of the inspection system [E10, E12]. Existing phone/photo support does not establish that relevant defects are visible or repeatably scaled. | Reuse photo suitability checks and viewer. Define the station's view, part placement, reference capture, lighting and visible scale; add targeted retake guidance after observing real failures. Mark uncalibrated size estimates clearly. | Which smallest relevant defects can two reviewers identify in the saved image? How do glare, blur, orientation, distance and lighting shifts affect decisions? |
| **6. Frozen factory evaluation and standard promotion gate** | This tests the central hypothesis and limits unmeasured regressions. Roboflow and Indurion already document evaluation/promotion controls [E5–E6, E15]. | Reuse paired comparison and benchmark tooling. Pin source data, labels, specification, model configuration and approved memory snapshot; reserve independent later parts; produce per-class errors, abstentions and latency. Require human review of results before a new standard affects the pilot. | Does a correction transfer beyond its source part without increasing escapes or false rejects? Which failure classes lack enough real examples to support any conclusion? |
| **7. Complete inspection evidence packet** | A disputed result should be reconstructable even after the specification, memory or model changes. Traceability is already an established proposition [E7–E9, E15]. | Extend existing JSON/CSV export and history with source/crop/mask references, identifiers, exact spec and approved-rule snapshots, model/configuration version, original result, human disposition, timestamps and relevant failures. Resolve storage/retention expectations first. | Can a quality lead reconstruct why a selected unit received its result and who changed it, using only the retained record? What evidence is actually required by the factory's existing quality process? |
| **8. Pilot operational health and safe interruption state** | Hosted calls, failed recall, pending memory saves and network interruption can stop a station. Edge competitors make continuity explicit [E11, E14, E16]. | Reuse visible service failures, pending-sync status, jobs and timing. Define inspection availability, missing-evidence states, retry behavior and duplicate prevention; keep an unresolved unit pending human handling. Do not make a failed service call an automatic acceptance. | What does the operator do when capture, network, vision or memory fails? What end-to-end delay and downtime can the station tolerate, and is hosted operation compatible with those limits? |

## What not to build first

- **A generic workflow canvas or model-training platform.** Roboflow and LandingLens already cover broad composition and train/deploy workflows [E1–E3]. The pilot needs a constrained inspection and quality process.
- **Automatic line rejection or a universal PLC/MES connector library.** First establish visible coverage, latency, ground truth and safe failure behavior at one station. Choose one actual integration only if the factory's workflow requires it [E10, E16].
- **Full root-cause analysis, supplier analytics or a manufacturing data lake.** Instrumental connects multiple production signals and serialized lifecycle data [E7–E9]. SpecLens has not established the necessary process data or causal evidence.
- **A new dashboard or another generic export screen.** These already exist locally. Prioritize meaningful unit identity, disposition and reproducible evidence over more aggregate charts.
- **A synthetic-data expansion presented as factory validation.** Synthetic fixtures can help development; they do not establish performance on the chosen material, lighting and real failure modes. Overview's synthetic-training descriptions [E12] are capability claims, not independent validation of SpecLens.
- **Full enterprise SSO, complex permission matrices or multi-site fleet orchestration.** Use the smallest authenticated team/approval model that the actual pilot requires; do not compete with Indurion's entire deployment-governance scope [E14–E16].
- **Offline/edge architecture by default.** Determine whether the factory permits hosted photo processing and whether its latency/network limits fit. A hard offline requirement is a feasibility decision before further product work [E11, E16].
- **Claims of calibrated measurement, zero misses, immediate production readiness, novelty, or guaranteed ROI.** None follows from this research or the existing demonstration.
